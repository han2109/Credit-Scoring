As the banking sector has developed more rapidly than ever before, data-driven decision-making is paramount in credit risk management and lending strategy optimization. Banks are under greater pressure than ever before to deploy creditworthiness wisely as they develop the capability of collecting and utilizing detailed customer information as a method of mitigating default risk. It is one of the simplest processes of the function to determine whether a customer is high-risk or low-risk on the basis of credit history and profile details.
Credit rating is a mathematical problem where banks must use past data in order to estimate the probability of default by a customer. It's not an administrative task. It is a binary classification issue in which the borrowers are categorized as "good" (paying) or "bad" (high default probability), based on theoretical terms acquired in our syllabus. Three widely used machine learning algorithms Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost were applied and compared by us in attempting to address this issue. 
Due to its interpretability and capacity for linear relationship capture, logistic regression was chosen as the baseline model. In contrast, there are two ensemble methods with the ability of detecting intricate patterns and nonlinear relationships in data, which are Random Forest and XGBoost.
Concurrent employment of all three models generated a balanced paradigm of comparison in both predictability and interpretability. 
Moreover, this practice was in line with other subjects throughout the course, i.e., class imbalance, dealing with categorical variables, model evaluation by accuracy, recall, and F1-score metrics, and overfitting as well as model sensitivity to generalization. By using this approach, our team was able to develop effective prediction models and achieve valuable insights into how machine learning can be applied to assist in credit risk assessment for practical purposes.
